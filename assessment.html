Question 1

A student has 4 shirts and 3 trousers. How many different outfits can be created?

Question 2

A restaurant offers 5 meals and 4 drinks. How many meal combinations are possible?

Question 3

A password consists of one letter followed by one number. If there are 6 letters and 5 numbers available, how many passwords can be created?

Question 4

A student can choose one pen from 5 pens and one notebook from 3 notebooks. How many stationery combinations are possible?

Question 5

A traveller has 4 choices of transport to a city and 3 choices to return. How many travel arrangements are possible?

Question 6

A restaurant offers 4 starters, 6 main meals and 2 desserts. How many different three-course meals can be formed?

Question 7

A phone is available in 3 colours, 2 storage sizes and 5 case designs. How many different combinations are possible?

Question 8

A student identification code consists of one letter followed by two digits. If there are 5 letters and 4 digits available, how many different codes can be created?

Question 9

A student chooses one Mathematics topic from 3 options, one Science topic from 4 options and one ICT topic from 5 options. How many possible selections are there?

Question 10

A student owns 3 bags, 2 pairs of shoes and 4 jackets. How many complete outfit combinations are possible?
