Practice Questions

Question 1

Clothing Combination

A student has 4 shirts and 3 trousers. How many different outfits can be created?

Multiply the number of choices from each category.

Solution

Number of shirts = 4
Number of trousers = 3

Fundamental Counting Principle:
Total outcomes = 4 × 3
= 12

Therefore, 12 different outfits can be created.

Question 2

Meal Selection

A restaurant has 5 meals and 4 drinks. How many different meal combinations are possible?

Multiply the meal choices by the drink choices.

Solution

Number of meals = 5
Number of drinks = 4

Total outcomes = 5 × 4
= 20

Therefore, 20 meal combinations are possible.

Question 3

Complete Outfit

A student has 3 caps, 4 shirts and 2 pairs of shoes. How many complete outfits can be created?

Multiply all independent choices together.

Solution

Caps = 3
Shirts = 4
Shoes = 2

Total outcomes = 3 × 4 × 2
= 24

Therefore, 24 complete outfits are possible.

Question 4

School Stationery

A student can choose 5 different pens and 3 different notebooks. How many stationery combinations are possible?

Multiply the number of pen choices by notebook choices.

Solution

Pens = 5
Notebooks = 3

Total outcomes = 5 × 3
= 15

Therefore, 15 stationery combinations are possible.

Question 5

Travel Routes

A person can travel to a town using 4 different buses and return using 3 different buses. How many possible travel arrangements are there?

The trip has two independent choices: going and returning.

Solution

Going choices = 4
Returning choices = 3

Total outcomes = 4 × 3
= 12

Therefore, 12 travel arrangements are possible.

Question 6

Password Creation

A password contains one letter followed by one number. If there are 6 letters and 5 numbers available, how many passwords can be created?

Multiply letter choices by number choices.

Solution

Letters = 6
Numbers = 5

Total outcomes = 6 × 5
= 30

Therefore, 30 passwords can be created.

Question 7

Food Menu Selection

A restaurant offers 4 starters, 6 main meals and 3 desserts. How many different three-course meals can be created?

There are three independent choices. Multiply all three choices.

Solution

Starters = 4
Main meals = 6
Desserts = 3

Total outcomes = 4 × 6 × 3
= 72

Therefore, 72 different meals can be created.

Question 8

Student Identification Code

A school creates identification codes using one letter followed by two numbers. If there are 5 letters and 4 numbers available, how many different codes can be created?

The number choices are repeated twice.

Solution

Letter choices = 5
First number = 4
Second number = 4

Total outcomes = 5 × 4 × 4
= 80

Therefore, 80 identification codes can be created.

Question 9

School Timetable Choices

A student chooses one subject from 3 mathematics topics, one science topic from 4 options, and one ICT topic from 2 options. How many possible subject combinations are there?

Multiply the choices from each subject category.

Solution

Mathematics = 3 choices
Science = 4 choices
ICT = 2 choices

Total outcomes = 3 × 4 × 2
= 24

Therefore, 24 subject combinations are possible.

Question 10

Mobile Phone Customization

A phone can be customized with 3 colours, 2 storage sizes and 4 accessories. How many different phone designs are possible?

Each customization option is an independent choice.

Solution

Colours = 3
Storage sizes = 2
Accessories = 4

Total outcomes = 3 × 2 × 4
= 24
